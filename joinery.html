Philosophy / 02

The work
between boards.

Joinery is where a piece earns its integrity. It determines how it carries weight, responds to the seasons, and stays useful long after the first finish has worn in.

A finished cabinet can look simple from across the room. The real story is usually at its corners, inside its frame, and beneath its surface, where one board meets another.

More than a way to hold things together.

Fasteners are useful tools, and they have a place in furniture. But when they become the whole structure, a piece depends on hardware to do work that wood itself can do beautifully. Traditional joinery shapes two parts so they lock, resist racking, and spread force through the material. Glue reinforces that relationship; it is not asked to carry it alone.

Why hand cut?

Hand cutting is slower, but it allows each joint to be fitted to the board in front of it. Wood is not perfectly identical from one piece to the next. A carefully tuned fit respects that variation and leaves the maker responsible for every surface that meets.

Dovetails: strength in the shape.

The flared geometry of a dovetail mechanically resists being pulled apart. That makes it especially well suited to casework and drawers, where the joint must endure the repeated force of opening, closing, and carrying weight. A visible dovetail also tells the truth about how a piece is built. It is a structural detail that has earned the right to be seen.

Mortise and tenon: a quiet framework.

A mortise-and-tenon joint pairs a projecting tenon with a matching cavity, creating a long-grain connection that works especially well for frames, legs, rails, and chairs. Its value is not spectacle. It is composure: a connection that can keep a table square and stable while allowing the material to respond to humidity over time.

Joinery is not decoration added to furniture. It is the furniture, expressed at the places where it matters most.

A tradition carried forward.

GouMang’s work draws from Chinese and Japanese woodworking traditions, where joinery is often treated as both engineering and language. The aim is not to imitate historical forms for their own sake. It is to use time-tested ways of thinking about grain direction, proportion, tolerance, and repair in pieces made for contemporary homes.

Built to be maintained.

Thoughtful construction gives a piece a future. A well-made joint can be inspected, tightened, repaired, or refinished by a skilled craftsperson. That does not mean it will never need care. It means care is part of the object’s long life, rather than a reason to discard it.
